        On the Structural Instability of Objective Persistence in
                       Self-Contained Systems
                                            Elias Arden
                                       Independent Researcher

                                                  Abstract
         This paper studies objective persistence in self-contained and self-amending systems, with
     particular attention to the distinction between actual persistence and assurance of persistence.
     The central question is not whether a system can optimize, nor whether self-modification is
     inherently unsafe, but under what conditions a claim that an objective has persisted can be
     supported by evidence that remains valid under the same amendment authority capable of
     changing the objective and its evaluation basis.
         We retain a minimal system description and add an assurance layer containing the semantics
     used to identify objective continuity, the mechanism that generates persistence evidence, and
     the basis that validates that evidence. Self-containedness alone is not treated as implying
     universal mutability or impossibility of formal verification. Instead, we identify a narrower
     failure condition: if an amendment authority can jointly alter the objective, the semantics
     of objective identity, and the validation basis in a way that preserves a positive persistence
     verdict while violating persistence relative to a fixed reference semantics, then that verdict is not
     amendment-independent evidence of persistence.
         The result yields two structurally distinct routes to defensible assurance. Either the admissible
     amendment set is constrained by a verified invariant that excludes such masking amendments, or
     at least one persistence-relevant validation basis remains outside the amendment authority being
     assessed. That basis need not be physically external; it may be an internal immutable kernel,
     a protected verifier, or an independently controlled external mechanism. The paper therefore
     establishes an assurance-independence condition, not a universal impossibility theorem and not
     a claim that any particular anchor is sufficient for long-run objective persistence.


1    Introduction
Advanced AI systems may update policies, learned representations, evaluation machinery, tools, or
portions of their own software. This creates a familiar concern: even if a system currently pursues
an intended objective, what supports the claim that the objective will remain the same over future
transitions?
    The strongest version of this concern can be overstated. Internal location does not by itself
imply mutability, and mutability does not by itself imply unverifiability. A self-contained system
may contain immutable components, and self-modifying code can in some settings be subject to
formal verification [9]. Likewise, a proof may be generated by an untrusted producer and still be
independently checkable under a validation policy that the producer does not control [8, 10]. The
issue studied here is therefore narrower.


                                                      1
    The paper asks when a persistence claim loses independent evidential force because the same
amendment authority can change both the object of assurance and the standards by which that
object is judged. This is a structural assurance problem. It is distinct from the empirical question of
whether objective drift will in fact occur, and from the engineering question of which implementation
is safest. In the title, “structural instability” therefore refers to instability of the evidential basis
under co-amendment, not to an assertion that every objective in every self-contained system must
drift.
    The contribution is threefold. First, we distinguish objective persistence, continued objective
pursuit, and assurance of objective persistence. Second, we formalize amendment authority over
both system and assurance components. Third, we state a conditional result: when an admissible
amendment can mask a persistence violation by co-amending the relevant semantics or validation
basis, an internally positive verdict is not independent evidence of persistence. The result does not
require a physically external human or institution. It requires either a verified invariant that rules
out masking amendments or a persistence-relevant basis that is not amendable by the authority
under evaluation.


2     Minimal Formal Framework
2.1      System state and assurance state
We model the operational system at time t by
                                    S(t) = {O(t), C(t), E(t), U (t)},                                 (1)
where:
• O(t) is the objective currently optimized or tracked;
• C(t) is the constraint structure under which optimization is carried out;
• E(t) is the operational evaluation criterion for task success or progress; and
• U (t) is the update rule governing admissible transitions to future states.
    To reason about claims of persistence, we additionally define an assurance state
                                       Q(t) = {I(t), G(t), V (t)},                                    (2)
where:
• I(t) specifies the semantics used to determine whether two objective representations count as
  the same objective for the declared assurance claim;
• G(t) is the mechanism that generates persistence evidence; and
• V (t) is the validation basis that determines whether the evidence supports the claim.
   The separation between S and Q is analytical rather than physical. Components of Q may be
implemented inside the same machine, process, organization, or software image as components of S.
Definition 1 (Objective persistence relative to a reference semantics). Let IR be a declared reference
semantics with equivalence relation ∼R . An objective exhibits persistence over an interval [t0 , t1 ] if
                                           O(t + 1) ∼R O(t)                                           (3)
for all relevant t ∈ [t0 , t1 ).

                                                   2
   The use of a declared reference semantics is deliberate. If the criterion for objective identity
changes at the same time as the objective, a later claim of “same objective” may otherwise become a
moving target rather than a statement about continuity relative to the original assurance question.

Definition 2 (Objective pursuit). Objective pursuit is the continued execution of updates or actions
directed toward the operative objective. Objective pursuit may terminate while the stored objective
remains unchanged. Conversely, pursuit may continue while the objective drifts.

Definition 3 (Self-contained system). A system is self-contained with respect to objective persistence
if its operational and assurance-relevant components are determined without reliance on an external,
non-optional reference that is required by definition for every admissible transition.

   Self-containedness does not imply that every internal component is mutable. A read-only kernel,
immutable hardware root, protected proof checker, or fixed transition invariant may be internal to
the system while remaining outside the amendment authority of other components.

2.2      Amendment authority
Definition 4 (Amendment authority). An amendment authority A is the set of transformations
that the system, agent, operator, or control domain under evaluation is permitted or technically able
to apply to designated components of S(t) ∪ Q(t).

   Let ReachA (s) denote the states reachable from state s under transformations admissible to A.

Definition 5 (Persistence-preserving amendment set). An amendment set is persistence-preserving
relative to IR if
                                ∀s′ ∈ ReachA (s), PR (s′ ) = true,                          (4)
where PR denotes the declared persistence property relative to IR .

   This definition permits strong internal guarantees when the admissible transition system itself
enforces an invariant. Self-modification is therefore not treated as sufficient for non-guarantee.

2.3      Assurance chains, roots of assurance, and masking amendments
Computer-security assurance distinguishes the component that makes a claim, the evidence used
to support it, the mechanism that appraises that evidence, and the trusted basis from which
appraisal begins. Roots of trust, trusted computing bases, reference monitors, proof-carrying code,
and remote-attestation architectures instantiate different versions of this separation. The present
paper adopts that structure for objective-persistence claims without assuming that any particular
implementation is sufficient.

Definition 6 (Persistence assurance chain). A persistence assurance chain for a claim relative to
IR is a tuple
                                     C = (M, G, V, B, D),                                     (5)
where:

• M is a measurement or observation process that binds evidence to the relevant system state;

• G generates evidence about the measured state;

• V appraises that evidence;

                                                  3
• B is the reference and trust basis used by V , including any appraisal policy, reference values,
  proof rules, trust anchors, or protected semantics required for validation; and

• D is the decision rule that turns the appraisal into acceptance, rejection, or an operational
  consequence.

The elements may be co-located or distributed. Their physical location is not what determines
assurance independence.

Definition 7 (Root of assurance relative to an authority). A component or set of components
B0 is a root of assurance for a persistence claim relative to amendment authority A if: (i) the
claim ultimately relies on B0 for validation, and (ii) A cannot silently amend B0 so as to preserve
acceptance while changing the persistence property being assessed. A root of assurance may be an
implementation of a root of trust, a protected proof checker, an immutable kernel, an independently
governed verifier, or another basis satisfying the same structural role.

Definition 8 (Masking amendment). A masking amendment for a persistence claim relative to IR
is an A-admissible transformation m such that:

 (i) persistence fails relative to the declared reference semantics after the transformation, and

(ii) the co-amended assurance chain nevertheless produces or validates a positive persistence verdict.

Formally, for some reachable state s′ = m(s),

                                              PR (s′ ) = false                                      (6)

while the resulting chain C ′ yields

                                   D′ V ′ G′ M ′ (s′ ) ; B ′        = PASS.                         (7)
                                                              


Definition 9 (Amendment-independent assurance). A persistence assurance is amendment-
independent with respect to authority A if its evidential validity cannot be preserved by an
A-admissible co-amendment that both violates the declared persistence property and rewrites the
assurance chain to continue accepting that violation.

    Amendment independence does not require that evidence be produced by an external party.
Proof-carrying code shows that an untrusted producer can supply evidence that a host independently
checks. Remote-attestation architectures similarly distinguish an Attester that produces Evidence
from a Verifier that appraises it using reference values and appraisal policies. The critical issue
is therefore not who emits the evidence, but whether the evidence is bound to the relevant state
and whether the rules, reference values, and trust basis that make it count as valid can be silently
rewritten by the same challenged authority.


3    Main Result
Lemma 1 (Internal location does not imply mutability). A component may be internal to a
self-contained system while remaining invariant under the admissible update relation.

Proof. Consider St = (a, bt ) with transition rule at+1 = at and bt+1 = f (bt ). The system state
evolves, but a is invariant. Thus state evolution does not entail that every internal component is
mutable.

                                                     4
   This lemma rules out an inference from “internal” to “mutable” without an additional assumption
about amendment authority.

Theorem 1 (Assurance-chain limit under admissible masking). Let A be an amendment authority
over a persistence-relevant system and assurance chain C = (M, G, V, B, D). Suppose:

(a) the declared persistence claim is evaluated relative to a fixed reference semantics IR ;

 (b) every element of the chain whose validity is required for acceptance, including the relevant
     appraisal policy or reference basis in B, is either directly amendable by A or depends for its
     validity only on components amendable by A; and

 (c) at least one masking amendment is admissible to A.

Then a positive verdict produced by that chain is not amendment-independent evidence of objective
persistence relative to IR .

Proof. By assumption (c), there exists an A-admissible reachable state s′ in which the declared
persistence property is false relative to IR while a co-amended assurance chain still yields PASS. By
assumption (b), the full acceptance path—measurement, evidence production, appraisal, reference
basis, and decision—contains no validation dependency whose integrity is protected from A or
grounded in a basis outside A’s effective amendment closure. Therefore the positive verdict cannot
distinguish the persistence-preserving trajectory from the admissible masking trajectory. A verdict
compatible with an admissible trajectory in which the declared property is false cannot, by itself,
provide evidence independent of the authority that controls both the challenged property and every
basis that makes the verdict acceptable.

Corollary 1 (Two routes to defensible persistence assurance). To defend an objective-persistence
assurance against amendment authority A, at least one of the following must hold:

 (i) the admissible amendment relation is constrained by a sound invariant or verification argument
     that excludes all relevant masking amendments; or

(ii) the assurance chain terminates in at least one persistence-relevant root of assurance whose
     integrity and semantics are not silently amendable by A.

    The two routes are not mutually exclusive. A protected verifier may enforce or check an invariant,
and a chain of trust may extend a protected root across mutable components when each successive
component is measured or verified before its result is relied upon. Conversely, a fully internal formal
system may supply a strong persistence guarantee if its transition semantics and proof basis exclude
the masking transformations relevant to the claim.

Proposition 1 (Assurance-chain obligations). A root of assurance alone is insufficient for a current
persistence claim. A defensible chain must additionally establish, for the declared scope, that:

 (i) the evidence is bound to the system state and component whose persistence is being claimed;

(ii) the evidence is sufficiently fresh for the decision being made;

(iii) the appraisal policy and reference values correspond to the declared reference semantics IR ;

(iv) intermediate mutable components cannot bypass the protected validation path without detection;
     and

                                                  5
(v) the final decision does not silently replace the declared persistence claim with a weaker surrogate.
    These obligations mirror mature assurance practice: roots of trust provide a starting point, chains
of trust extend protection transitively, reference-monitor designs emphasize complete mediation
and tamper resistance, and remote attestation separates evidence production from appraisal and
reliance. None of these mechanisms automatically proves objective persistence; they supply stronger
engineering patterns for constructing the independent assurance chain required by the corollary.
Remark 1 (Physical externality is not required). The corollary does not imply that a persistence
basis must be physically external to the system. An internal read-only kernel, hardware root of trust,
protected proof checker, or other component outside the relevant amendment authority may satisfy
the structural independence requirement. Conversely, an external service controlled by the same
amendment authority may fail to provide independence.
Remark 2 (Necessity is conditional, not sufficient). A basis outside amendment authority A may
be necessary for amendment-independent assurance in cases where masking amendments cannot
otherwise be excluded. Its existence is not sufficient to guarantee long-run objective persistence. The
basis may be incorrect, incomplete, unavailable, bypassable, or irrelevant to other failure modes.


4     Failure Modes: Persistence, Pursuit, and Assurance
Earlier formulations can conflate distinct failures. Relative to the definitions above, three broad
failure classes should be separated.
Proposition 2 (Three distinct failure classes). The following are conceptually distinct:
(1) Objective drift: O(t + 1) ̸∼R O(t) for at least one relevant transition. This directly falsifies
    objective persistence relative to IR .
(2) Pursuit termination: the system ceases active optimization or action toward the operative ob-
    jective. This ends objective pursuit but does not, by itself, imply that the objective representation
    has changed.
(3) Assurance collapse: the available evidence or validation basis can no longer establish whether
    persistence holds, or becomes co-amendable in a way that permits masking. This undermines
    assurance but does not, by itself, prove that objective drift has occurred.
   This separation matters because “not persistent,” “no longer pursuing,” and “cannot indepen-
dently establish persistence” are different claims and require different evidence.


5     Boundary Cases and Counterexamples
The revised result is intentionally compatible with several cases that defeat a broader impossibility
claim.

5.1   Internal immutable kernel
Suppose a system contains an internal kernel K whose transition semantics are not amendable by
authority A, and K enforces
                                  ∀s′ ∈ ReachA (s),    PR (s′ ) = true.                              (8)
Then self-containedness alone does not block a persistence guarantee. The system follows route (i)
of the corollary, and K may also serve as a basis satisfying route (ii) relative to A.

                                                   6
5.2   Self-modifying but verified transition system
A system may modify executable code while remaining subject to a verified invariant over admissible
state transitions. The relevant question is not whether code changes, but whether the proof and
transition semantics actually exclude a masking or persistence-violating reachable state.

5.3   Untrusted producer with independent checker
An evidence producer may be untrusted and may generate both code and a proof. If a receiving
checker validates that proof against a safety or persistence policy the producer cannot amend,
evidence can remain independently checkable. Evidence provenance and validation authority are
therefore distinct.

5.4   External but non-independent service
Physical separation is insufficient. If an external service, auditor, or validator can be reconfigured by
the same authority whose persistence claim it evaluates, external placement alone does not create
amendment independence.


6     Related Work
The argument is adjacent to several strands of AI safety, formal verification, and computer security
research.

Goodhart-type failures and specification gaming. Goodhart-type analyses study divergence
between optimized metrics and intended targets [5]. Reward tampering and specification gaming
study ways in which evaluation channels or proxies can be manipulated [1, 4]. The present paper is
narrower: it concerns the evidential status of a claim that the objective itself has persisted when
the object, semantics, and validation basis may be jointly amendable.

Mesa-optimization and learned objectives. Mesa-optimization research studies learned
optimizers whose internal objectives may diverge from training objectives [3]. That literature
addresses mechanisms of objective emergence and divergence. The present result does not predict
such divergence; it specifies an assurance boundary for claims of continuity under amendment
authority.

Corrigibility and human intervention. Corrigibility research asks whether systems remain
amenable to correction or shutdown [6, 2]. Objective persistence and corrigibility are neither identical
nor necessarily aligned: preserving an objective can be desirable in one context and harmful in
another. The present paper therefore treats persistence as an assurance property, not as a universal
safety goal.

Self-modifying agents. Formal work on rational self-modifying agents shows that self-
modification does not automatically imply goal instability [7]. Under specific evaluation conventions,
utility modification may be harmless or disfavored. This supports the narrower formulation used
here: self-modification, by itself, is not an impossibility premise.




                                                   7
Proof-carrying code and foundational verification. Proof-carrying code demonstrates that
an untrusted producer may supply code together with evidence that a host validates under its own
safety policy [8]. Foundational proof-carrying code pushes the trusted base downward toward a
smaller set of axioms, a simpler checker, and a smaller runtime substrate [10]. Work on certified
self-modifying code further shows that runtime code mutation can be compatible with mechanized
formal verification [9]. These results directly motivate the separation between evidence production
and control over the validation basis.

Roots of trust, trusted computing bases, and chains of trust. NIST describes roots of
trust as highly reliable hardware, firmware, or software components that perform critical security
functions and provide a foundation from which trust can be built [12]. NIST platform-resiliency
guidance further requires chains of trust to be anchored in roots of trust and protects roots and
chains against unauthorized modification [13]. The present paper adopts the same structural lesson
without assuming hardware implementation: a persistence claim needs a protected starting basis
or a verified invariant, and mutable downstream elements must inherit trust through verifiable
transitions rather than through self-assertion.

Reference monitors and security kernels. The reference-monitor tradition requires the policy-
enforcement mechanism to be always invoked, tamper-resistant, and sufficiently small or analyzable
to be verified [11]. Security kernels implement this concept within a trusted computing base. For
persistence assurance, the analogous requirement is that no amendment path capable of changing
the objective or its meaning can silently bypass the validation path on which the persistence claim
relies.

Remote attestation and evidence appraisal. The IETF RATS architecture separates an
Attester that produces Evidence, a Verifier that appraises that Evidence using appraisal policies,
reference values, and endorsements, and a Relying Party that acts on Attestation Results [14]. It also
emphasizes evidence freshness, binding evidence to the intended target environment, and protection
of appraisal policies and reference values. This provides an established engineering vocabulary for
the assurance chain used here. Objective persistence is a more specialized claim than platform state
attestation, but the separation of measurement, evidence, appraisal, reference values, and reliance is
directly applicable.


7    Scope and Limitations
Several limitations are important.
    First, the theorem is conditional. It does not state that every self-contained or self-modifying
system admits masking amendments. A verified transition system may exclude them.
    Second, the theorem concerns amendment-independent assurance, not the empirical probability
of objective drift. A system may remain stable for long periods without independent assurance, and
it may fail despite apparently independent assurance if the assurance basis is unsound or incomplete.
    Third, the result does not establish that any particular human, institutional, hardware, or
cryptographic anchor is necessary. Physical externality is neither required nor sufficient. The
relevant property is independence from the amendment authority under evaluation, or an invariant
strong enough to rule out the relevant masking amendments.
    Fourth, the result does not specify a universal reference semantics IR . In practice, functional
equivalence between objectives may itself be difficult to define, especially for learned or distributed


                                                  8
representations. This is a substantive modeling burden rather than a solved component of the
framework.
    Fifth, the result is not a safety theorem for advanced AI systems. Objective persistence can
preserve a harmful objective, and changing an objective can sometimes be desirable. The paper
addresses the validity of persistence claims, not whether the persisted objective is aligned, beneficial,
or safe.
    Sixth, every independence claim is relative to a declared amendment authority A. If the threat
model omits a higher-level operator, supply-chain actor, signing-key holder, verifier owner, or other
party able to modify the supposed root of assurance, the analysis may overstate independence. A
root of assurance is therefore a trust assumption to be minimized, protected, and independently
scrutinized, not an automatically trustworthy object.
    Seventh, attestation or proof of software state is not automatically proof of semantic objective
continuity. Measurements must actually cover the persistence-relevant representation, reference
semantics, and update path. A perfectly authenticated measurement of the wrong property does
not establish objective persistence.
    Eighth, route (i) depends on model adequacy as well as proof soundness. A verified invariant
excludes masking amendments only relative to the transition system, semantics, and threat model
actually formalized. If the deployed implementation admits transitions omitted from the model,
or if the compiler, proof checker, hardware semantics, or other trusted substrate is unsound, the
formal result need not transfer to the real system. This is another reason to minimize and expose
the trusted base rather than treating “formally verified” as a self-sufficient label.
    Finally, the result does not establish empirical prevalence, calibration, or operational thresholds.
Its role is conceptual: to separate persistence, pursuit, and assurance, and to identify the structural
conditions under which a positive persistence verdict ceases to be independent of the authority
capable of changing what that verdict is meant to certify.


8    Conclusion
Objective persistence in self-contained systems should not be framed as a universal impossibility
result. Internal components can be immutable, self-modifying systems can be formally verified
under suitable models, and evidence may be generated by an untrusted producer while remaining
independently checkable.
    The narrower structural problem arises when amendment authority extends simultaneously
over the objective, the semantics used to identify continuity, and the validation basis that accepts
persistence evidence. If that authority admits a masking amendment—a reachable transformation
that violates persistence relative to the declared reference semantics while preserving a positive
internal verdict—then the verdict is not amendment-independent evidence of persistence.
    Defensible assurance therefore has two general routes: constrain the admissible amendment set
with a sound invariant that excludes masking transformations, or construct an assurance chain that
terminates in a protected root of assurance and preserves state binding, freshness, non-bypassability,
and semantic correspondence along the path to acceptance. The protected basis need not be
physically external. What matters is that the challenged amendment authority cannot silently
rewrite the basis or the chain so as to preserve PASS after violating the declared property.
    This reframing preserves the foundational question while aligning it with mature assurance
engineering. Objective persistence may be possible, and self-modification may be formally verified,
but a persistence claim cannot acquire independent evidential force merely because the same
authority that can change the objective can also produce a chain of evidence whose measurement


                                                   9
rules, appraisal policy, reference values, and acceptance criteria it can silently redefine. The
strongest formulation is therefore not an “external anchor” theorem but an assurance-independence
requirement grounded in protected trust bases, verified invariants, and auditable chains of evidence.
